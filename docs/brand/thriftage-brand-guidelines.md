# Thriftage Brand Guidelines

## Positioning

Thriftage is the fashion-first social resale marketplace where discovery adapts to personal style. It brings together real wardrobes, personalized feeds, grounded outfit ideas, protected conversation, and a simple way to sell pieces that deserve another life.

The primary audience is Gen Z and young professionals in Pakistan: thrift shoppers, university students, closet sellers, small resellers, and early fashion creators. The system remains global-ready by avoiding location-specific visual clichés and using PKR only where the product currently demonstrates Pakistan launch behavior.

## Brand promise and tagline

**Primary tagline:** Style finds you here.

The line expresses personalized discovery without overclaiming automation. It works for the website, social creative, pitch material, and app-store campaigns.

Secondary campaign lines:

- Find what feels like you.
- Your closet has another life in it.
- Better pieces. Longer lives.
- Your style. Your feed. Your next find.

## Personality

Thriftage is stylish, confident, warm, editorial, youthful, smart, social, and trustworthy. It is premium enough to trust but approachable enough for an everyday closet. Sustainability is a natural benefit, not a guilt campaign.

## Voice

Write short, human, fashion-aware sentences. Lead with the customer outcome. Use confident verbs such as **find**, **style**, **save**, **sell**, and **wear**. Explain intelligence through what it does, not how it is engineered.

Do:

- “Find pieces that actually feel like you.”
- “Ask for an occasion, budget, color, or style.”
- “Every recommendation comes from eligible Thriftage inventory.”

Don’t:

- use AI, sustainability, or startup jargon;
- promise perfect fit, guaranteed safety, income, or public availability;
- overuse slang, exclamation marks, or luxury-snob language;
- invent users, sellers, testimonials, or growth metrics.

## Logo

The mark is a continuous-line **T** with a central tag notch. The single move suggests a garment tag and a second life without literal recycling arrows. It must stay simple enough for a favicon and app icon.

Use the horizontal lockup in headers and formal brand contexts. Use the standalone mark for avatars, favicons, app icons, and compact navigation. Maintain clear space equal to roughly half the mark width. Do not rotate, stretch, add gradients, place it on noisy photography, or rebuild it as a hanger, shopping bag, or leaf.

Approved source assets live in `apps/web/public/brand/`. The app icon uses an ivory mark on Primary Forest with generous mask-safe padding.

| Coral Deep     | `coral-deep`  | `#C94C2E` | Hover/pressed coral states                 |

Forest and ivory should dominate. Coral is punctuation, not a second background system. These values intentionally match the established mobile marketplace tokens.

Use Coral Deep—not Coral—as the background behind ivory button text so normal-size CTA labels meet WCAG AA contrast. Coral remains the brighter accent for marks, rules, and large display details.

## Typography

- **Display:** Fraunces Variable, with Georgia and serif fallbacks. Use selectively for editorial marketing headlines.
- **Body/UI:** Manrope Variable, with Arial and sans-serif fallbacks. Use for navigation, forms, product details, and long copy.
- **Wordmark:** uppercase Manrope-style geometric sans with generous tracking.

Use sentence case for headlines. Avoid decorative serif text in controls or dense UI. Keep body copy at a comfortable line length and never reduce legibility for fashion styling.

## Image direction

Use real Thriftage demo product imagery and populated app screens. Photography should be tactile, modern, diverse, and product-led: fabric, folds, everyday wardrobes, and clean editorial still lifes. Avoid generic smiling stock models, luxury runway imagery, neon/cyberpunk styling, visible third-party logos, and fictional customer photography presented as proof.

Device frames should be consistent and crop Android status/navigation chrome. Product screenshots remain truthful to the staged application; conceptual brand references are design specifications, not customer evidence.

## UI connection

The public site translates the mobile system rather than recreating it. Shared cues are forest, coral, warm neutrals, rounded commerce surfaces, restrained shadows, product photography, and strong hierarchy. The site may use more whitespace, larger editorial typography, and varied section rhythm.

Use focused primary actions, visible focus states, semantic structure, and reduced-motion fallbacks. Do not turn the site into a repetitive feature-card grid.

## Social and campaign use

Use the mark alone for avatars. Pair the primary tagline with one product or app moment in social cards. Campaign copy may use the secondary lines above, but should keep the same promise: personal discovery, real resale inventory, and a beautiful way to sell.

The beta is invitation-led. Use “Join the beta,” “Get early access,” or “Apply as a seller.” Do not use app-store badges or “Download now” until public listings exist.
